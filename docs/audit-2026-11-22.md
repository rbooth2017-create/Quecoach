# Cue Coach — full audit (22 November 2026)

**Goal:** be the best DJ software for working DJs, with game play on top that teaches younger DJs.

**How I checked:** ran the Learn app (`learn.html`) in a browser at 1440×900 and 1920×1080 with the practice tracks, played the beginner game loop, switched to Pro and the full decks, opened DJ Life, measured the layout with scripts, read the code behind each finding, and ran the test suite (286 of 286 pass). Branch `learn-app`, including the uncommitted start-screen work. No controller and no rekordbox helper were connected, so controller and rekordbox behaviour is from the code only.

---

## Bottom line

1. **There is no real "no game" pro setup yet.** The game is woven into every view. "Just the decks" still shows the bedroom-to-main-room career, the streak, stars and gig progress. Pro level in the Play view still coaches ("Tap Faster"). The start screen asks about DJ Life on every launch. Level (how much help) and mode (game or not) are the same switch today, and they need to be two.
2. **The pro decks are too small to use.** With the side panel open at 1440×900, the decks are scaled to 65%. 106 of 110 text labels on the decks render under 9 px, and 120 of 175 visible buttons are smaller than 24 px. At 1080p they're still at 81%, with empty space under them. A working DJ can't read this in a dark booth.
3. **The game has two careers that contradict each other.** With DJ Life on, the top-left says "Bedroom at your parents'" while the bottom-right says "Headline set. Keep that floor full." Pick one spine.
4. **Anyone can skip the game by picking a level.** Choosing Pro in the level menu moves you straight to the Main room. For kids, that makes the career meaningless.
5. **The pro feature set has gaps a working DJ will hit in the first hour:** keyboard control of deck A, hot cues and loops; tempo ranges; a master limiter and meter; waveform zoom; real settings. Details in section 1.3.

The teaching side is strong and still unmatched. The beginner Play view is the best part of the app: clear, fun and distinctive. The work now is to give pros their own clean room, and to give the game a single, earned progression.

## 1. Pro decks, no game

### 1.1 Separate "mode" from "level" — P0

Today `js/levels.js` decides both how much coaching you get and, through `syncToLevel` in `js/career.js`, where you are in the career. The Play view is on at every level (`playView: [all four]`). So there's no state that means "I'm a DJ, just give me decks".

What I saw with "Just the decks" chosen at Pro:

- The header still shows **Play view**, **Main room** and the level chip.
- The Play view at Pro still shows the match circle, "Up next is a little slow. Tap Faster." and gig progress, though the Pro blurb says "No coaching".
- In the full decks, the lesson bar ("Meet your decks · step 3 of 4") shows because the side pane is open (`renderCoach`: `show = f.lesson || state.paneOpen`).
- The start screen ("Start DJ Life / Just the decks") appears on every launch, even after picking "Just the decks" many times.

**Recommendation:** add a top-level **Mode** with two values, chosen on first run and in Settings:

| | **Learn** (game) | **Pro** (no game) |
|---|---|---|
| Opens on | Play view or DJ Life | Full decks, last set loaded |
| Levels | Starter → Pro, earned through the career | Hidden. Everything on |
| Career, streak, stars, gig progress, crowd | On | Off and hidden |
| Lessons, tour, coach bar, toasts that teach | On | Off (Help still has them) |
| Start screen | Yes | Never |
| Play view button | Yes | Hidden (available in Settings) |

Mode should be one flag that `featuresFor()` reads alongside the level, so there's still one table deciding what's on screen.

### 1.2 Layout of the full decks — P0

Measured at 1440×900 with the side panel open:

- `.decks-area` zoom **0.645**. Effective text is 6.5–8.4 px ("CUE" 7.1 px, "Start" 6.5 px, "+0.00%" 7.1 px, pad labels 7.1 px).
- **The side panel overflows the window by about 46 px** (`.sp-form`, `#setEvent`, `.tutor-head` end at x = 1462–1486 in a 1440 window). The Refine/"No…" buttons, the event description and the energy chart are cut off on the right.
- At 1920×1080 the decks are still zoomed to 0.81, and the deck cards don't stretch, so there's a band of empty space between the decks and the library.
- The jog wheels take most of each deck card, while the tempo fader, CUE, pads and loop controls are tiny. Mouse and keyboard users rarely touch the jog; controller users don't need it on screen at all.
- The "full decks" look (flat grey) is a different visual language from the Play view's neon. It reads as two products.

**Recommendation:** a pro layout designed for 1280–1920 wide, without zoom:

1. Stacked, full-width scrolling waveforms at the top (the beat lane already does this). Add zoom (−/+ and scroll).
2. Deck strips: track info, big BPM and key, tempo fader, play/cue, 8 pads. Jog wheels small or hidden, with a setting.
3. A compact mixer at full size type (12 px minimum, 32 px hit targets).
4. Library takes the rest of the height, as in every pro app.
5. The side panel pushes the stage over rather than overflowing. Fix the `.sp-form` width first, as it's a plain bug.

### 1.3 What a working DJ will look for

✅ there · 🟡 partial · ❌ missing

| Area | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Key lock | ✅ | Signalsmith Stretch in an AudioWorklet |
| Quantise, hot cues, loops (¼–32), beat jump | ✅ | |
| Beat FX, pad FX, sampler, filter | ✅ | |
| Recording, mix report | ✅ | |
| Headphone cue on a 4-output controller | ✅ | Untested on Windows (WASAPI) per the competitive review |
| **Keyboard control** | 🟡 | Only Q/P play, and speed and nudge for **deck B**. Nothing for deck A's tempo, CUE, hot cues, loops, EQ or loading. No remapping. Mouse-and-keyboard pros need a full map (like the rekordbox/Serato keyboard sets) |
| **Tempo range** | 🟡 | ±8% or ±16%, and tied to level. Pros expect ±6/10/16/50% per deck |
| **Master output safety** | ❌ | No limiter, no master meter, no clip light. Master goes to 1.25 (`MASTER_MAX`) and two loud decks plus FX can clip the recording and speakers |
| Waveform zoom | ❌ | |
| Key shift (manual semitones) | ❌ | The maths is in `deckmath.js` |
| Slip mode, reverse, censor | ❌ | Expected by scratch and open-format DJs |
| Booth output | ❌ | |
| Session history / export (tracklist for a set) | ❌ | |
| Settings | 🟡 | The Settings dialog is a list of shortcuts to other dialogs. No audio buffer/latency, tempo range, load behaviour, jog mode, recording format, or default view |
| Sync | ❌ by design | The rule "never add a sync button" is right for Learn. In Pro mode, working DJs will expect it. That's your call, not a bug. If it stays out, say so up front in Pro mode |
| Controllers | 🟡 | Full support for the DDJ-FLX4 only; basic maps for 7 others; no MIDI learn in the Learn app |
| 4 decks, stems, streaming | ❌ | Fine to defer |

### 1.4 Release blockers for pros (unchanged from the competitive review)

- **Licence risk:** `js/controllers.js` still says it's generated from Mixxx's GPL-2.0-or-later mappings. Rebuild it from manufacturer MIDI lists before any paid or closed-source release.
- **Unsigned installers:** macOS and Windows warnings.
- **Latency test on Windows** with key lock on both decks has not been done.

## 2. Game play

### 2.1 What works

- The step strip **Pick → Match → Ready → Hide it → Start → Fade in → Bass → Finish** makes the order of a mix obvious.
- "Lock the pulse" (two circles that overlap as the speeds match, with a %) is a lovely, readable metaphor.
- The crowd's mood in plain words ("Your mates are nodding along") and the phase bar ("Same bar of the phrase ✓") are good live feedback.
- One click to pick a song, with "Perfect / Good" match labels in Play Next.
- DJ Life's isometric room is charming and runs smoothly.

### 2.2 Issues

| # | Issue | Severity | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | **Two careers at once.** The core venues (bedroom → main room, `js/career.js`) and DJ Life (money, rent, homes) both run. Seen together: "Bedroom at your parents'" with "Headline set. Keep that floor full." | P0 | When DJ Life is on, it owns progression: hide core gig progress and the venue chip, and let DJ Life venues set the level |
| G2 | **The level menu skips the career.** `setLevel` → `syncToLevel` moves you to that level's venue. Picking Pro puts a beginner in the Main room | P0 | In Learn mode, levels above your venue are locked ("Get booked at the Warehouse to unlock"). Going down stays free, for practice |
| G3 | **Pro level in the Play view still coaches.** Match % and "Tap Faster" stay on. The Pro blurb says "No coaching" | P1 | At Confident and Pro, hide the speed hint and match %, and use the "check" reveal the full decks already have |
| G4 | **Short progression.** 3 mixes at 2+ stars per venue (`GIG`) means 12 good mixes from bedroom to main room, with the same task every time | P1 | Give each gig a brief tied to a skill: "bass swap on the phrase", "stay in key", "build the energy", "echo out". Add a daily challenge and a by-ear mini game |
| G5 | **The score ignores track choice.** Keys count for 5%; energy and crowd fit count for nothing (`PARTS` in `mixreport.js`). Reading a room is half of DJing | P1 | Add a "track choice" part (key, energy step, crowd taste from DJ Life) and show it in the report |
| G6 | **Tempo taps are tiny.** One tap of Faster moved the gap from 0.63 to 0.57 BPM, so about 10 taps for a typical gap | P2 | Bigger first step, or hold to ramp |
| G7 | **Camelot codes at Starter.** The song picker shows "11A", "8A" badges with no explanation, while other Starter views use plain-word match labels | P2 | Hide the badge at Starter, or a tooltip "Key: songs with close numbers blend well" |
| G8 | **DJ Life intro drawn twice on first launch.** `chooseLaunch` asks for `openRoom` (draws the intro) and the pack's own 600 ms timer draws it again. The card jumped position and my first clicks on "Go to your room" didn't register. I couldn't make it happen again after that | P2 | Skip the timer when the start screen already opened the room |
| G9 | **Young users and money/links.** The app links out to Spotify, Apple Music and "buy it", and asks for an Anthropic API key. If the game is aimed at under-16s, this needs a plan (UK Age Appropriate Design Code, COPPA in the US) | P1 before launch | A "young DJ" profile with no external links, no API key entry and no purchase prompts; get advice on the rules |

## 3. Design

### 3.1 Strengths

- The Afterhours look is distinctive and doesn't copy rekordbox or Serato.
- Colour tokens and six themes, including "Match my computer".
- Every one of the 197 visible controls has an accessible name, and the dials are keyboard sliders with `role="slider"`.

### 3.2 Issues

### Not done, on purpose

- **Controller maps from manufacturer MIDI lists (1.4).** This needs Pioneer's published MIDI documents and real hardware to verify. Making up tables would be worse than the current GPL-derived ones. Still a release blocker for a paid build.
- **Sync in Pro mode.** Your product decision, not a bug. Pro mode still has no sync.
- **Signed installers, Windows latency test.** Need certificates and hardware.
- **Splitting `learn.js`.** New features went into new modules (`prefs.js`, `keymap.js`, `history.js`) instead of growing it further; a full split is a separate, risky refactor best done on its own branch.
- **By-ear mini game.** The by-ear mode at Confident/Pro and the daily challenge cover part of it; a stand-alone ear-training game is still open.

### Not checked

- With a real controller or a 4-output sound card (booth output, limiter with the headphone socket route).
- Audio quality of key shift on real music (the stretcher is the same one key lock uses).
- The desktop (Electron) build.
